Spine-type check · experimental

Which spine type might you be?

Roussouly sorts spines into four types, mainly by the angle of the sacrum, the bone at the base of your spine. This check estimates that angle from the skin over your sacrum in one side-on photo, then shows which type range it falls in.

Experimental, not a diagnosis. Roussouly types are measured on X-ray, from the bone. A photo only shows skin, and nobody has yet shown how closely the skin angle matches the X-ray angle. Treat the result as a conversation starter. If you have back pain or leg symptoms, see a physio or GP.

01 · STAND

Side-on, relaxed

Stand as you normally do, arms folded in front so they don’t hide your back. Don’t pose or suck in.

02 · SHOW THE SACRUM

Neck to buttock crease

Bare back, with your waistband lowered a few centimetres so the flat bony area above the buttocks and the top of the crease are visible.

03 · CAMERA

Level, at hip height

About 2 m away, straight side-on and not tilted. A tilted phone tilts the result, so prop it on something level or use a timer.

    Type 1 or Type 2?

    Both have a low sacral angle, so the shape above it decides. Type 1: a short, low hollow just above the buttocks, with the rounding of the upper back running down towards the waist. Type 2: little hollow and little rounding, so the whole back looks fairly straight. Compare your traced line with the drawings.

    How it works

    Roussouly’s classification (Roussouly et al., Spine, 2005) is based on the sacral slope: the angle of the top of the sacrum on a standing side-on X-ray. Below 35° the lower back tends to be flatter (Types 1 and 2), 35–45° is the balanced Type 3, and above 45° is the deep curve of Type 4.

    An X-ray measures the top surface of the sacrum. This check measures the slope of the skin over the back of the sacrum instead, which is a stand-in, not the same thing. The back of the sacrum isn’t exactly square to its top surface, and soft tissue varies from person to person. That’s why it’s labelled experimental.

    To make it better, I’m looking for people who know their Roussouly type from an X-ray to try it. Message @theflatbackguy if that’s you.
