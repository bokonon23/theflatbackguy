Spine-shape check · beta

Is your back flatter than most?

A two-minute curiosity check from one side-on photo. You tap four points along your back and it estimates how curved your lower and upper back look.

Not a diagnosis. Only an X-ray can measure your spine type. Photos are affected by clothing, posture and camera angle. If you have back pain or leg symptoms, see a physio or GP.

01 · STAND

Side-on, relaxed

Stand as you normally do, arms relaxed by your sides or folded in front. Don’t pose or suck in.

02 · FRAME

Neck to buttocks

Bare back or a fitted top. The whole back from neck to buttocks must be in the photo.

03 · CAMERA

Level, at hip height

About 2 m away, straight side-on, not tilted. Ask a friend, or use a timer.

Your photo stays on your phone

The check runs entirely in your browser. Your photo isn’t uploaded, sent or stored anywhere, and it’s gone when you close the page.

    How it works

    Your four points mark the base of the neck, the most rounded point of the upper back, the deepest point of the lower back and the back of the buttocks. The check fits a curve through each set of three and works out how many degrees it bends. A flat back gives small angles, because there’s little curve to measure.

    Skin and muscle sit over the spine, so a photo shows the shape of your back, not your bones. Use it to get curious, then read about the four spine types.
