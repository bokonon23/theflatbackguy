Sitting with a flat back

For flat backs and sciatica sufferers, not a chair for everyone. My sciatica starts almost as soon as I sit down. This is the journey towards a solution that may not exist yet.

Why sitting is hard for a flat back

Sitting flattens every spine, and a flat back runs out of curve first. With the hips bent to about 90°, the pelvis rolls backwards and the lower-back curve drops. X-rays show the curve falls significantly in unsupported sitting compared with standing (Andersson et al. 1979).

A typical spine starts with curve to spare. A Roussouly Type 2 spine starts with little, so ordinary sitting tips it past flat into flexion: the lower back rounds the wrong way. A low pelvic incidence also limits how far the pelvis can roll forward to win the curve back.

So the problem isn’t posture discipline. It’s geometry: a 90° hip angle on a flat seat asks a flat back for a curve it doesn’t have.

Slump, arch or supported?

Neither a slump nor a forced arch. The aim is a mild curve held by the chair, so muscles can rest, plus frequent changes of position.

OptionWhat happensFor a flat back
SlumpThe pelvis rolls back and L5/S1 bends like a hinge: the front of the disc is squashed and the back is stretched open, next to the nerve root (Snijders et al. 2004)Worst for long periods, at exactly the level of past trouble
Forced archActively sitting up straight raised disc pressure by about 10% over relaxed sitting in one person (Wilke et al. 1999), and needs constant muscle effortNot sustainable; there’s little arch available
Supported mild curveThe chair holds the pelvis upright, so muscles and ligaments restThe target

A caution on the slump: in Wilke’s single subject, slouched sitting gave the lowest disc pressure. The case against slumping is the sustained stretch on the back of the disc, the ligaments and the nerve, not overall pressure.

The café position

The one way I can sit for longer: leaning forward with my elbows on the table. Three things seem to help:

  • The pelvis tips forward with the trunk, instead of rolling back underneath me.
  • The arms take load. Resting the forearms carries weight the back muscles would otherwise hold.
  • The knees are bent, which takes some tension off the sciatic nerve.

No study I’ve found measures this directly, so it’s mechanics and my own experience, not evidence. My test: minutes until sciatica starts, elbows down against elbows up, repeated on different days.

The Makhsous study tested 25 people with low back pain and 10 without. It didn’t measure the lower-back curve, and nobody has tested it on flat backs.

The sciatic nerve problem

Taking weight off the sit bones moves it onto the back of the thighs, which is where the sciatic nerve runs. In 31 cadavers the nerve passed through the middle third of the gap between the sit bone and the outer hip bone (Ma et al. 2024), then down the middle of the back of the thigh.

So a chair that frees the sit bones mustn’t simply move that load onto the centre line of the thighs. No study I’ve found measures sciatic nerve pressure against seat shape. That gap is the opening.

The floating chair: a concept

The idea: leave the sit bones and the centre line of the thighs free, carry the body on the outer thighs from underneath, block the sacrum from behind, and tilt the seat 5–10° forward so the pelvis can roll upright.

ZoneSupport or reliefWhyRisk to solve
Sit bonesRelief (drop or gap)Lets the pelvis roll forwardThe pelvis may sink or rock

Support, don’t squeeze. Gripping the sides of the thighs would have to hold body weight by friction alone, so the squeeze would be far larger than the load. Supporting the underside of the outer thighs carries weight directly.

The closest published design is a four-part flexible seat pan (Park et al. 2025). It improved comfort and reduced back-muscle fatigue, but it was tested only on healthy students, and two authors work for companies selling the chair. It says nothing yet about flat backs or sciatica.

The first prototype will be cheap: a forward wedge, two foam strips under the outer thighs, a gap down the middle and a rolled towel at the sacrum. Twenty minutes at a time, logging any leg tingling. Results will go up here.
